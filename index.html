Lo/Be Lab

Lo/Be Lab designs environments that help people reflect their way through life transitions: building the chooser, not steering the choice.

It is a design-based research practice. It works through spatial, narrative, and computational environments, and through AI that reflects a person's own material back to them instead of giving advice.

Established 2024. Founded and led by Seth Looper, an architect, Project Manager, Stewardship, at Yale University, and Adjunct Faculty at Kent State University's College of Architecture and Environmental Design.

This site is the lab's public working notebook. Each project has a working report: built with students and revised across multiple academic terms.

The methods, instruments, and working reports, failures included, are public and free to use and cite. Write to the lab with questions, citation requests, or pushback.

Projects

  • Negotiating Selfhood with AI Working paper · Jiaxi Pan with Seth Looper · November 2026 How Chinese international students use conversational AI to reflect on their goals and sense of self. Bilingual survey and interviews, led by the lab's research assistant.
  • Inclusive Leadership Lab Single delivery · Dartmouth · May 2026 A two-hour workshop reframing inclusive leadership as anchor + hold space, for international undergraduate peer mentors. Delivered once.
  • DartWorld Concluded, v6 · Dartmouth · 2024–2026 A spatial narrative platform with reflective onboarding, 3D environment, and longitudinal journaling. Rebuilt each term, 2024–2026.
  • Synapse Active, v2 · 2024–present An exploration instrument that replaces the ranked list with a spatial map. 13 questions, 460 occupations from BLS & O*NET.
  • Career Design Lab Concluded · Dartmouth · 2024–2026 Six tools woven into a term-long deliberation system. Each term was a design-iteration cycle.
  • Narrative by Design Concluded · Dartmouth · 2024–2026 A 90-minute workshop built around narrative compression: paragraph → sentence → single word. Paired with card sorts and an AI-as-mirror.
  • Threshold Active, v4 · Independent · 2025–present Discipline-specific career toolkit for architecture, landscape architecture, and urban design.

Open questions

  1. Can designed environments and guided conversation help people reflect during life transitions at the scale of a full undergraduate cohort, beyond a single session?
  2. What design principles help spatial, conversational, and computational tools support people in seeing, reframing, and making sense of their own stories?
  3. Does personal reflection make people better at group decision-making? Can tools that help individuals understand themselves also improve how communities talk to each other?
  4. How can AI surface evidence about a person without replacing their own judgment about what it means?
  5. What's specific to a discipline, and what's universal, when designing reflection tools for a particular profession?
  6. Who decides which technologies mediate student development, on what knowledge, and what would it take to choose and design them well?
