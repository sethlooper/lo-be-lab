Design cycles, not curricula

What changes when you treat teaching as design-research iteration.

A curriculum is something you deliver. A design cycle is something you run.

The shapes look similar, both run on a term clock, both have a syllabus, both end somewhere. The orientation is different.

A curriculum is fixed at the start. The syllabus is the contract. You can't change a lecture mid-term without breaking that contract; improvements wait until next year. The frame is service delivery: you are providing an education, and the students are receiving it.

A design cycle is, by definition, revisable. The students know it is a cycle. The cycle's premise is that I do not yet know the right shape of the work, and that figuring out the right shape is part of what the term is for. Improvements happen inside the term, not just between terms.

My Dartmouth projects, CDL most clearly, ran in design-cycle mode at Dartmouth (2024–2026). The six tools changed in every direction across multiple cycles: an exercise got added, the order of two tools got swapped, a prompt got reworded after I noticed an unexpected pattern in the room. Each cycle showed me what to drop and what to keep.

This changes how the work is documented:

  • Instructor field notes are kept on every meeting; some of them become these notes.
  • Redesign drew on those field notes. Student artifacts were not collected or analyzed as research data.
  • A "this didn't work" is a design finding. The term ends with a revision list, not a debrief.

The risk: students who expected a predictable curriculum (rare but real) get something they didn't sign up for. The program handled this by being explicit about the design-cycle frame from the first session: this is a working tool, you are participating in its development, the format may change midway. Most students lean into this once they understand it. A few don't, and that's worth noting in its own right.

Running teaching as design cycles is more work. Every term you're rebuilding pieces. The payoff is tools that improve with each generation, but the trade only works if your institution lets the syllabus stay unfinished, and many institutions don't.
