Compression as a method

A method that keeps showing up across my projects, often unannounced.

The method is compression: take a messy thing, cut it down, force a choice.

Narrative by Design is the most explicit case. The workshop's center is a sequence: paragraph → sentence → single word. Each step strips something out and forces the student to decide what to keep. The word at the end works as a decision tool.

The Career Design Lab did the same thing in its OneWord tool, but the surrounding sequence is six tools long. Identity Mapping Studio starts wide: every experience, sorted across four dimensions. By the time the program reaches OneWord, the cohort has been compressing for weeks. The single word is the last act of a long pull-down.

Threshold inherits compression from somewhere else entirely: architectural studio critique. A studio crit is itself a compression exercise, five minutes to defend three months of work, which means deciding which two ideas are the project. Threshold ports that move from design education to career deliberation.

The shared structure is worth naming. In each case, compression is forced prioritization with a witness. The student would not, on their own, decide that this experience matters more than that one. The deadline (90 minutes, one card, one word) does the deciding for them. They produce, then defend, then re-do.

The card-sort tools that recur in the lab (Knowdell Career Values, a strengths sort informed by CliftonStrengths themes, Knowdell Motivated Skills, all in NBD) are also compression, choose your top 10, then your top 5, then your top 3. Maybe compression is my core method, and everything else is a domain-specific application of it.

The pair-witness piece keeps coming up. In facilitation I have noticed that solo compression tends to produce abstractions that sound impressive and mean little, while compression in pairs tends to produce specific, often uncomfortable, language; this has not been studied. The peer is doing some work the format alone can't do. A side-by-side comparison of solo and paired compression was never run, and CDL concluded in 2026. If the difference is real, the practical implication is that compression tools shouldn't be built as solo apps.
