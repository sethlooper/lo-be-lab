About the lab

An applied research and design practice building environments where people can hear themselves think.

Lo/Be Lab grew out of one observation: the places meant to help people through life transitions (universities, career centers, advising offices) were built for throughput, not understanding. So we build environments, spatial, narrative and conversational, where people can get their thinking out in the open and see what is there.

Everything starts as a prototype. We put it in front of real students, watch what happens, and redesign. Each term was a design-iteration cycle, and each project is written up as a working report.

One commitment we won't argue: career development is a form of learning, not a service-delivery problem. Everything else is open to revision. See research for the questions and publications for the papers.

How we work

  • Research, not consulting We don't deliver a workshop and leave. We review what we make, and what we learn changes what we make next.
  • Field-tested, not theoretical Our tools have been used with real students in real institutions, term after term. The design rationale comes from the field.
  • Environments, not advice We don't tell people what to do. Spatial interfaces, narrative workshops and conversation systems slow people down and give them room to reflect.
  • A working notebook This site is the public edge of the lab: methods, instruments and reports, failures included.

Principles

  • Mirror, not advisor AI and instruments reflect a person's own words back to them. Judgment stays with the person.
  • Build the chooser, not the choice We design spaces for people to do their own thinking.
  • Longitudinal, not one-shot Reflection tools are designed for whole terms and cohorts. The trajectory is the unit.
  • Reflection before action Job search and skill-building come after a person has something true to act on.
  • Open by default Nothing here is for sale; everything here is for use, citation or pushback.
  • Honest about confidence Every claim says how far the evidence goes. Open questions are named.
