About the lab

An applied research and design practice building environments where people can hear themselves think.

Universities, career centers and advising offices are mostly built to move people through life transitions quickly. The lab builds spatial, narrative and conversational environments where people can get their thinking out in the open and see what is there.

Lo/Be Lab is Seth Looper, working with one research assistant. Everything starts as a prototype. Each prototype goes in front of students, and what happens shapes the next version. Each term was a design-iteration cycle, and each project is written up as a working report.

The lab treats career development as a form of learning. Its other positions are open to revision. See research for the questions and publications for the papers.

How the lab works

  • Research practice A workshop is not delivered and left behind. Each one is reviewed afterward, and the review changes the next version.
  • Built in use The tools were used with students over multiple terms. The design rationale comes from that use; it has not been formally studied.
  • Environments, not advice The work doesn't tell people what to do. Spatial interfaces, narrative workshops and conversation systems slow people down and give them room to reflect.
  • A working notebook This site is the public edge of the lab: methods, instruments and reports, failures included.

Principles

  • AI as mirror AI and instruments reflect a person's own words back to them. Judgment stays with the person.
  • Autonomy-preserving design The environments are designed so people do their own thinking and reach their own conclusions.
  • Longitudinal Reflection tools are designed for whole terms and cohorts. The trajectory is the unit.
  • Reflection before action Job search and skill-building come after a person has something true to act on.
  • Open by default Nothing here is for sale. Methods, instruments and reports are free to use, cite or contest.
  • Stated confidence Every claim says how far the evidence goes. Open questions are named.
